U·four·ia: A game you should play

Hebe's got nothing on Bop-Louie. (Or so I've been told.)

Intro

U·four·ia: the Saga is a 1993 game for the NES. Developed by Sunsoft, it's pretty good! It features four characters, a pretty gigantic world, and a pretty rockin' soundtrack! Bop-Louie is our...okay, so this game is actually kinda weird.

U·four·ia was originally known as Hebereke, the first game in a series called...Hebereke. (Very creative, Sunsoft.) Somewhere down the line, this game was released in Europe with a pretty interesting localization that removed a lot of the humor in Hebereke. I figure that it would be cool to talk about the differences. Only problem is...I don't speak Japanese.

(That's right, this is a bait and switch. This isn't an article about a cool game you should play. This is about localization.)

What to do now?

So, how I am going to talk about this game and the regional differences if I don't speak Japanese? Good question! I will refer to an online translation. Created by BMF54123, it attempts to be a mostly accurate preservation of the original's style. Some things had to be dropped (google Japanese first-person pronouns, there are LOTS. Plus some are archaic and all have different connotations.) but overall, I think it does well. U·four·ia 2 carries over the personality traits from the original, and the fan translation of the first game seems to match well. I trust it enough for this article.

Personality

In Hebereke, every character had a different personality. Hebe (the penguin dude) was sort of childish in his speech and movement, O-chan (the girl in a cat costume) was haughty, Sukezaemon (the ghost guy with glasses) talked like a samurai, and Jennifer (the fish-like dude [yes, he is a guy]) kind of acted like a skater dude. A very weird cast of characters all around. The localization changed ALL of that. Now, everyone talks kind of stilted. Their personalities are gone. Hebe, O-Chan, Sukuzaemon, and Jennifer were renamed to Bop-Louie, Freeon-Leon, Shades, and Gil. Hebe and O-Chan got facelifts too. See for yourself!

In addition, the story was altered a lot. In Hebereke, the story was very self-aware and bizarre: It involved Hebe trying to get back to his homeworld. The other three characters were only there to help him get back. (I think they all resided in the world of Hebereke.) The game ends with Hebe flying off in his rocket...only for him to come right back! Turns out, this WAS his homeworld after all. Bizarre. (I guess he had an off day.)

In U·four·ia, this was changed: Now, all four characters are from a place called U·four·ia. This new place is completely unfamiliar. Bop-Louie needs to find his friends (who have all forgotten him for some strange reason) and get them all out of there. At the end, Leon suggests they all stay there. Everyone except for Louie agrees. He rockets off...only to come back, saying he had such a great time that he wants to live there. Bizarre once again, this time for entirely different reasons.

Little changes

Sometimes the most fascinating things about localizations are the little changes. In most NES games, it came down to getting rid of religious imagery, nudity, and gore. Stuff that would be acceptable to Nintendo of Japan sometimes wouldn't fly with Nintendo of America.

That being said, I don't think a bird dropping poop on someone is particularly crass, but it was changed. In Europe, birds drop 16-ton weights I guess. Also, one enemy got shorter arms for...some..reason. Honestly, not much to talk about here.

What could have been

One last note: apparently, the game was supposed to get a release in the US. There is a prototype version of U·four·ia made for the US. It has a ROUGH translation, rougher than even the final European version. Also, the game map is bigger (the final game had to be downsized in order to fit into a smaller amount of memory) and there is a simple map legend. (There's even a cut pyramid location!)

Honestly, this game should've been brought to the US. It apparently had a pretty troubled development, and the final game could've been a lot bigger and more varied. One only imagines what could've been...
